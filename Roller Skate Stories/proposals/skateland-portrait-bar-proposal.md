## Executive Summary

Roller Skate Stories (RSS) proposes a **Portrait Bar add-on service** for Skateland Chandler's daytime events — birthday parties, organization outings, school field trips, and family sessions. The service gives event hosts professional, shareable photos of their guests, while creating a new revenue stream for Skateland and a concrete job pipeline for local skaters.

This proposal focuses on the **daytime business** because that is where Skateland already has strong bookings. The goal is to turn every daytime event into a content and revenue opportunity that can help fund future nighttime adult-skate marketing.

---

## What Is the RSS Portrait Bar?

The Portrait Bar is a **premium but affordable photo activation** built from the same gear and workflow as GenIdem, a Phoenix-area portrait-booth service run by RSS-aligned operators. It is closer to a mini studio portrait setup than an iPad selfie booth.

**Guest experience:**
1. A small portrait area is set up at the event.
2. Guests step in, press a handheld trigger, and take their own photo.
3. A professional strobe and camera deliver high-quality, flattering images.
4. Within 24-72 hours, the host receives a private online gallery link.
5. Guests scan a QR code at the event to access the gallery once it is live.

## Why This Fits Skateland Daytime Events

Skateland's daytime revenue comes from:
- Birthday parties
- Family skate sessions
- School and organization field trips
- Other group bookings

These events all have something in common: **people want proof they were there.** Parents want photos of the birthday party. Schools want memories from the field trip. Organizations want content for newsletters and social media.

Right now, those guests leave with only a memory. The Portrait Bar closes that gap — and gives Skateland a way to monetize it.

## Proposed Service

A simple add-on for birthday party hosts: **one flat fee for unlimited Portrait Bar access during their entire party**.

| Package | Coverage | Party Host Fee | Deliverables |
|---------|----------|----------------|--------------|
| **Party Portrait Bar** | Full party | **$150** | Unlimited guests photographed during the party; private online gallery; 24-72 hour delivery |

The package includes:
- Portrait Bar equipment setup and breakdown
- On-site operator from RSS or the Portrait Bar team
- Professional camera, lighting, and handheld trigger
- Online gallery with downloadable images
- QR code card for guests to access the gallery

**Pricing is simple:** one flat fee for unlimited access during the party. No separate setup fee.

### Space & Power Requirements
- **Minimum space:** 10' × 10'
- **Location:** Ideally near a power outlet, though a portable power station is available if needed.
- **Setup time:** RSS needs access to the space shortly before the party begins.

Optional add-ons for future consideration:
- Instant on-site printing
- Branded backdrop or Skateland-branded overlay
- Same-day gallery delivery
- Short event recap video reel

## Business Relationship

This proposal involves two separate business relationships:

1. **GenIdem Portrait Bar ↔ RSS** — RSS contracts the Portrait Bar service from GenIdem at a backend rate.
2. **RSS ↔ Skateland** — RSS offers the Portrait Bar as an add-on to Skateland's birthday party bookings.

The **party host** is the paying customer. They pay Skateland (or RSS, depending on booking flow) for the Portrait Bar as part of their party package.

## Partnership Model

Skateland promotes the Portrait Bar as an add-on to its birthday party bookings. For each party host that adds the Portrait Bar, **Skateland keeps 10% of the booking fee** as a referral/partnership commission. The remaining 90% is split between RSS and the Portrait Bar team.

- **Party host fee:** $150
- **Skateland commission:** $15 per booking (10%)
- **Remaining $135:** Split between RSS and the Portrait Bar team

This keeps the pitch simple for Skateland: no upfront cost, no operational burden, and a direct financial incentive to offer the add-on. RSS and the Portrait Bar team handle equipment, operator, gallery delivery, and customer communication.

### Example Booking Flow
1. Parent books a birthday party at Skateland.
2. Skateland pitches the RSS Portrait Bar as a $150 add-on.
3. Party host pays Skateland for the party package + Portrait Bar together.
4. After the event, RSS invoices Skateland for the net amount ($135 per booking).
5. Skateland keeps the $15 commission.
6. RSS settles the remaining $135 with the Portrait Bar team.

### Payment Cadence
Options for Skateland to remit the net amount to RSS:
- **Per event:** Settled at the end of each event day.
- **Weekly:** Aggregated payment once per week for all bookings that week.
- **Bi-weekly:** Aggregated payment every two weeks.

The preferred cadence can be set once we understand Skateland's existing party-payment workflow.

## Operator, Liability & Cancellation

- **Operator:** Each event is staffed by either an RSS media team member or a trained operator from the Portrait Bar team.
- **Liability:** RSS assumes responsibility for Portrait Bar operation and any damage caused by the Portrait Bar equipment or operator during the event.
- **Cancellation:** RSS will align its cancellation policy with Skateland's existing party cancellation policy.

## What the RSS Media Team Does

| Role | Responsibility |
|------|----------------|
| **Setup / Operator** | Transports gear, sets up the Portrait Bar, stays nearby during the event, troubleshoots. |
| **Editor / Gallery Manager** | Culls and edits images, uploads gallery, sends link to host. |
| **Posting / Promotion** | Creates short recap clips or photo highlights for RSS and Skateland social channels (with permission). |

Initially, roles may be filled by volunteers or by RSS team members building portfolio and experience. As revenue grows, these become paid positions.

## Pilot Plan

To prove the concept before scaling:

1. **Pilot Event:** One scheduled birthday party or organization event at Skateland Chandler.
2. **RSS provides:** Equipment, operator, gallery delivery, and a simple one-page flyer explaining the service.
3. **Skateland provides:** Space, power access, and a test booking to offer the add-on.
4. **After the pilot:** Photos from the party are delivered to the host. Both sides review host feedback, revenue, and operational friction.
5. **Decision:** Move to a standing partnership, adjust pricing, or refine the offering.

## Open Questions to Resolve

### Partnership & Legal
- Photo release / usage rights: Can RSS, Portrait Bar, and connected parties use event photos for marketing and business purposes? Should this be in a signed contract?

### Booking & Sales
- How are birthday parties currently booked? (Phone, website, in-person, third-party platform?)
- When in the booking flow can the Portrait Bar be pitched? (At booking, confirmation call, day-of check-in?)
- Who pitches it? (Front desk, party host coordinator, manager, automated email?)
- Is there a current list of à-la-carte add-ons? Where does the Portrait Bar fit in that list?
- Can Skateland add a checkbox to its online booking form?
- What is the deadline for a host to add the Portrait Bar before the event?
- What happens if a host books late? Can RSS still be mobilized?

### Pricing & Payment
- Is $150 the right price point for Skateland's customer base?
- Does Skateland want to mark it up, or stick with the 10% commission model?
- Who collects payment from the host? (Skateland at booking, or RSS after the event?)
- If Skateland collects, how and when does it remit the $135 to RSS?
- What payment methods can RSS accept? (Check, ACH, Venmo, etc.)
- What is the invoicing cadence? (End of day, weekly, bi-weekly?)
- Are there refunds if the host cancels the Portrait Bar add-on?

### Scheduling & Confirmation
- How far in advance is each party confirmed? (1 week, 48 hours, day-of?)
- How does RSS get notified of a booked Portrait Bar event? (Email, shared calendar, text, Slack?)
- Who is the Skateland point of contact for day-of coordination?
- What is the latest time RSS can confirm or decline an event?

### Pre-Event Prep
- Who provides the final headcount and party timing?
- What time can RSS arrive to set up? (30 min before? During the prior session?)
- Can RSS scout the space before the first event?
- Where exactly is the 10'×10' space allocated?
- What if the ideal space is unavailable day-of?

### Day-of Operations
- Where does RSS load in gear? (Back door, front entrance, loading dock?)
- Is there parking for the RSS operator?
- Who unlocks the space and lets RSS in?
- Can RSS set up while another party is still in the room?
- Does the operator stay for the full party, or only a window?
- What if the party runs late? Is there an overtime rate?
- What if the host wants the Portrait Bar in a different location than planned?
- How does RSS handle guest line management if it gets busy?
- What if a guest declines being photographed? Does RSS have a release process?
- Are minors involved? Is parental consent handled by the host or by Skateland?
- Does RSS need a badge, lanyard, or vendor identification?
- Who does the operator report to on-site if there is an issue?

### Post-Event
- When does teardown happen? Immediately after the party, or after guests leave?
- How quickly does the operator need to clear the space?
- How are photos delivered to the host? (Email with gallery link, text, handed a card?)
- How long is the gallery live?
- Who handles host questions or complaints about photos?
- What if the host wants a refund because of technical issues?

### Content & Marketing
- Can RSS and Skateland use anonymized or permissioned photos to promote the Portrait Bar?
- Can RSS film B-roll of the Portrait Bar in action for social media?
- Does Skateland want co-branded content, or does RSS post independently?
- Should RSS provide Skateland with one-sheet/flyer assets to help front desk pitch it?
- Can RSS put up a small sign or QR display at the rink to advertise the service?

### Pilot & Scale
- Which event type should be the pilot? (Birthday party, organization event, field trip?)
- How many pilot events before deciding to scale?
- What does success look like for Skateland? (Revenue per event, host satisfaction, rebooking rate?)
- What does success look like for RSS? (Booking volume, operational smoothness, host feedback?)
- How does Skateland track which party hosts were pitched and which booked?
