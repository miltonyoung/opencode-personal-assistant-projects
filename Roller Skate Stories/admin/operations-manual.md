# Roller Skate Stories — Operations Manual

**Version:** 1.0-draft  
**Owners:** Marcus Dover (primary), Milton Young (secondary)  
**Goal:** Turn interest into organized action. Build an administrative layer so owners plan and delegate, while contributors execute.

---

## 1. The operating model

RSS has six public lanes from the “Get Involved” brochure. Those lanes become the internal **functional areas** that people are assigned to.

| Lane | What RSS offers | Why it needs contributors |
|------|-----------------|---------------------------|
| **Movement** | Skate presence, community outreach, lessons/teaching pipeline | Needs people who show up, teach, welcome newcomers |
| **Media** | Photos, videos, stories, editing, posting, profile content | Needs shooters, editors, writers, social managers |
| **Merch** | Apparel, mugs, branded items | Needs designers, production coordinators, sellers |
| **Music** | Soundtrack, vibe, DJ/curator relationships | Needs DJs, curators, music coordinators |
| **Events** | Meetups, adult nights, rink partnerships, birthday add-ons | Needs planners, promoters, on-site leads |
| **Positivity** | Culture, hospitality, mentoring, conflict support | Needs greeters, mentors, community guardians |

Each lane has a **Lane Lead** who owns the lane’s output. The two owners (Marcus and Milton) sit above the lanes as **strategic owners**, not as daily doers.

## 3. Task lifecycle

Every piece of work moves through these stages. The Operations Coordinator owns the board; the Lane Lead owns the quality; the Contributor owns the execution.

```
Idea / Request
     ↓
Triage (Owner or Lane Lead)
     ↓
Ready to Assign
     ↓
Assigned to Contributor
     ↓
In Progress
     ↓
Submitted for Review
     ↓
Approved / Rejected → if rejected, back to In Progress
     ↓
Done + Archived
```

### Task rules

- Every task has one **owner** (the doer), one **requester**, and one **deadline**.
- Every task is tied to a **lane**.
- Tasks smaller than one sitting are broken down.
- Contributors choose from the “Ready to Assign” pool; Lane Leads can also directly assign.
- Nothing sits in “In Progress” for more than one week without a status update.

## 4. Onboarding flow

When someone fills out the “Get Involved” form, this is what happens next.

### Step 1: Capture (Operations Coordinator)

- Add their name, contact, interests, and availability to the **contributor roster**.
- Tag them with the lane(s) they selected.

### Step 2: Welcome (within 48 hours)

- Send a short welcome message with:
  - What RSS is
  - How we work (this manual)
  - Link to the task board / communication channel
  - Ask them to confirm their top 1–2 lane interests and weekly time availability

### Step 3: Orientation call or async packet

- Share the operations manual
- Explain the lane system and badge roles
- Ask them to pick a starter task from the “Good First Task” list

### Step 4: First task

- Assign a small, low-risk task that can be finished in one sitting.
- Example first tasks:
  - Write 3 Instagram captions for a recent event
  - Find 2 local DJs on Instagram and message them with a script
  - Attend one skate session and take 10 photos
  - Update the merch idea spreadsheet with 5 item ideas

### Step 5: Check-in

- After the first task, the Lane Lead or Operations Coordinator checks in.
- If it went well: assign a slightly larger task.
- If it did not: understand why, realign or release.

## 5. Weekly rhythm

| Day | Who | Action |
|-----|-----|--------|
| **Monday** | Operations Coordinator | Send weekly digest: what’s due this week, what’s stuck, what’s unassigned |
| **Mid-week** | Lane Leads | Contributor check-ins (5–10 min each) |
| **Friday** | Operations Coordinator + Lane Leads | 30-min review: completed tasks, blocked tasks, new tasks for next week |
| **Monthly** | Marcus + Milton | Strategic review: what lanes are working, what roles need to be filled, revenue/expenses |

## 6. Tool decision (pending)

No work-tracking tool is selected yet. The Operations Coordinator should research and recommend one within two weeks.

### Minimum requirements

- Free or low-cost
- Multiple users
- Tasks can be assigned, due-dated, and tagged by lane
- Mobile-friendly (skaters are on phones)
- Comment/attachment support

### Candidate categories

- **Simple boards:** Trello, Notion, ClickUp
- **Community-friendly:** Discord with task bots, Google Sheets + group chat
- **Project-focused:** Asana, Monday, Linear

Decision: Operations Coordinator presents 2–3 options with pros/cons, owners pick.
