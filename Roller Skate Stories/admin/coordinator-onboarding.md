# Coordinator Onboarding Guide

**For:** The first 2 organized people joining RSS as task coordinators / scrum leaders.  
**Goal:** Get them ready to run the task system so Marcus and Milton can focus on ownership.

---

## What a coordinator is

A coordinator is not a regular volunteer. They are an organizer of organizers.

Their job is to:
- Keep the task board current
- Make sure every task has an owner, deadline, and clear next step
- Check in with contributors when work stalls
- Run the weekly rhythm
- Spot blockers and raise them to the owners

They do **not** decide the vision, spend money, or approve partnerships. They keep the machine running.

---

## Coordinator responsibilities

1. **Task hygiene**
   - Every task has one owner, one requester, one lane, one deadline, and a definition of done.
   - Stalled tasks get a follow-up within 24 hours.
   - Completed tasks are moved to done and archived.

2. **Routing**
   - New requests from owners or Lane Leads are turned into proper tasks.
   - Tasks are matched to contributors based on their badges and availability.

3. **Weekly rhythm**
   - Send the Monday digest: what is due, what is stuck, what is unassigned.
   - Run the Friday review with owners and Lane Leads.
   - Capture lessons and update processes.

4. **Contributor care**
   - Watch for overloaded or quiet contributors.
   - Make sure new contributors get a first task and a check-in.

5. **Reporting**
   - Give Marcus and Milton a short weekly status: green/yellow/red by lane.

---

## What coordinators need from RSS

- Access to the task-tracking tool
- Access to the contributor roster
- A clear escalation path to Marcus and Milton
- Authority to assign tasks and set deadlines with Lane Leads
- Protection from being pulled into doing creative/event work themselves

---

## Coordinator onboarding flow

### Step 1: Personal welcome (Marcus or Milton)

- Call or message the person directly.
- Explain the role and why you want them.
- Confirm they are interested and have 2–4 hours per week.

### Step 2: Send the coordinator packet

Include:
- This onboarding guide
- The operations manual
- The mission-to-operations document
- The current tasks list
- Access to the task board once it exists

### Step 3: Orientation call

Topics:
- RSS mission and lanes
- The task lifecycle
- The weekly rhythm
- Decision rules and escalation
- Their first assignment

### Step 4: First assignment

Give them a concrete, bounded task that builds the system.

Example first assignments:
- Research and recommend a task-tracking tool
- Build the contributor roster from the form responses
- Draft the weekly digest template

### Step 5: Check-in and real assignment

After the first assignment, review together.
- What worked?
- What was confusing?
- What do they need to be effective?

Then hand them ongoing coordinator work.

## First assignments for the two coordinators

### Coordinator A: Task-tracking tool researcher

**Task:** Research Trello, Notion, ClickUp, Asana, Monday, or other options and recommend the best one for RSS.

**Why them:** They are organized and good at comparing tools.

**Deliverable:**
- 2–3 options evaluated
- Pros/cons for each
- Clear recommendation
- Setup instructions or a draft workspace

### Coordinator B: Contributor roster builder

**Task:** Compile the first contributor roster from the 20+ “Get Involved” form responses.

**Why them:** They are detail-oriented and good with people data.

**Deliverable:**
- Roster with name, contact, lane interests, skills, availability
- Top 6–10 candidates flagged for first outreach
- Notes on any promising coordinator candidates

---

## Boundaries

Coordinators can:
- Assign tasks
- Set deadlines
- Follow up with contributors
- Update the task board
- Run meetings

Coordinators cannot:
- Spend money
- Approve partnerships
- Change pricing or offers
- Promise anything to outside partners without owner approval
- Overrule a Lane Lead or owner decision

---

## Success at 30 days

- Task board is active and trusted
- Contributors are getting assignments and check-ins
- Owners are no longer chasing individual tasks
- Weekly rhythm is running smoothly
