# From Mission to Operations: How RSS Actually Works

**For:** Marcus Dover, Milton Young, and anyone who wants to understand Roller Skate Stories from the top down.  
**Goal:** Connect the big idea to the day-to-day work.

---

## 1. What Roller Skate Stories is

Roller Skate Stories is a home for the roller skating community.

It is not just a YouTube channel, a business, or an event group. It is the *organizing idea* that every skater — from day one to year thirty — belongs to something bigger than the session they just left.

Think of it like the Boys & Girls Club for skating: a place where people find belonging, family, security, and something to look forward to, just by showing up with their skates on.

---

## 2. The mission

> Roller Skate Stories is about preserving the heart of the culture: the people, the sessions, the struggles, and the joy — and giving skaters a home where their stories matter.

That mission has three parts:

1. **Preserve the culture** — record it, remember it, pass it on.
2. **Serve the people** — welcome skaters, connect them, support them.
3. **Make the stories visible** — so skaters see themselves as part of history.

Everything RSS does must touch at least one of those three parts. If it does not, it is not RSS work.

---

## 3. What RSS offers: the six lanes

These are the six ways RSS carries out the mission.

| Lane | What it is | How it serves the mission |
|------|------------|---------------------------|
| **Movement** | Getting people on skates, teaching, welcoming newcomers, building a pipeline of skaters and teachers | Serves the people — makes skating accessible and keeps the community growing |
| **Media** | Photos, videos, interviews, social posts, profiles, editing | Preserves the culture and makes the stories visible |
| **Merch** | Apparel, mugs, branded items that let people rep RSS | Makes the stories visible — turns members into walking reminders of the culture |
| **Music** | DJs, playlists, soundtracks, curating the vibe at events | Preserves the culture — music is part of the skating experience |
| **Events** | Meetups, adult nights, rink partnerships, birthday add-ons, games and attractions | Serves the people — gives skaters a place to belong and something to look forward to |
| **Positivity** | Hospitality, mentoring, community norms, conflict support | Serves the people — makes the home feel safe and welcoming |

Each lane is both a **public invitation** (“get involved here”) and an **internal function** (“this is how we operate”).

---

## 4. From lanes to work

Every lane has ongoing work. That work is what needs to be delegated.

### Movement

- Organize regular skate meetups
- Recruit and coordinate skate instructors
- Match new skaters with mentors
- Track who shows up and follow up with them
- Build relationships with local rinks

### Media

- Plan what to film or photograph
- Shoot sessions, events, and profiles
- Edit videos and photos
- Write captions, profiles, and story copy
- Post content and respond to comments

### Merch

- Collect ideas for items
- Design products
- Find vendors and handle production
- Track inventory and sales
- Ship or deliver orders

### Music

- Build DJ relationships for events
- Curate playlists for sessions and content
- Coordinate music rights and vibe

### Events

- Plan event structure, games, and attractions
- Book venues, DJs, and influencers
- Promote events
- Run day-of logistics
- Capture content during events for the Media lane

### Positivity

- Welcome newcomers at sessions and events
- Train greeters and mentors
- Uphold community norms
- Support people who feel left out or conflicted

## 5. From work to roles

If the work above is what needs to happen, the roles below are who does it.

### Owners

These are the people steering the ship.

| Role | Who it is today | What they do |
|------|-----------------|--------------|
| **Primary Owner** | Marcus Dover | Holds the vision, makes final decisions, handles partnerships and money |
| **Secondary Owner** | Milton Young | Runs operations, coordinates people, makes sure things keep moving |

The owners’ job is **not** to do every task. Their job is to make sure every task has a person and a path to completion.

### Operations Coordinator

This person owns the machinery of delegation:

- Maintains the task board
- Routes tasks to the right lane
- Checks deadlines
- Runs the weekly rhythm
- Follows up with contributors when things stall

Today this might be Milton. Over time it should be someone else so Milton can focus on strategy and partnerships.

### Lane Leads

Each lane has a lead who owns the quality and output of that lane.

| Lane Lead | Owns |
|-----------|------|
| Movement Lead | Skate meetups, teaching pipeline, attendance, rink relationships |
| Media Lead | What gets shot, edited, written, and posted |
| Merch Lead | Product ideas, designs, production, inventory, sales |
| Music Lead | DJs, playlists, event sound |
| Events Lead | Event planning, promotion, day-of execution |
| Positivity Lead | Welcome flow, mentors, community norms |

A Lane Lead does not have to do everything in the lane. Their job is to break the lane’s work into tasks and assign them to contributors.

### Contributor badges

These are the actual doers. A badge is a hat someone wears. One person can wear multiple hats.

| Badge | Typical work |
|-------|------------|
| Videographer | Film sessions and events |
| Photographer | Take photos at sessions, events, and portrait shoots |
| Video Editor | Cut and finish footage |
| Photo Editor | Cull, color correct, and deliver galleries |
| Writer | Write profiles, captions, and story copy |
| Social Poster | Schedule and post content, reply to comments |
| Event Assistant | Help setup, check-in, cleanup, and run errands |
| Event Host | MC, lead games, keep the energy up |
| Outreach Volunteer | Message rinks, DJs, influencers, sponsors |
| Designer | Make merch, flyers, decks, and other assets |
| DJ / Music Curator | Play or build playlists for events |
| Greeter / Mentor | Welcome newcomers and pair them with mentors |
| Skate Tech Liaison | Connect people to mobile skate repair/maintenance |

## 6. How work moves: the task lifecycle

This is the simple path every task follows.

```
Idea / Request
     ↓
Triage (Owner or Lane Lead decides: is this real? is it urgent?)
     ↓
Ready to Assign
     ↓
Assigned to a Contributor
     ↓
In Progress
     ↓
Submitted for Review
     ↓
Approved or Rejected (if rejected, it goes back)
     ↓
Done
```

The rule is: **every task has one owner, one requester, one deadline, and one lane.** If any of those are missing, the task is not ready to assign.

This stops work from floating around unnamed and unowned.

## 7. How someone goes from “interested” to “contributing”

Right now RSS has 20+ people who said they want to help. This is the path for each of them.

### Step 1: Capture their information

Name, contact, interests, availability, skills.

### Step 2: Welcome them

Within 48 hours, send a message that says:
- Thank you
- What RSS is
- Link to this document
- How we work
- Ask them to confirm their top 1–2 lane interests

### Step 3: Orient them

Share the lanes, roles, and how tasks work. Help them pick a badge.

### Step 4: Give them a first task

Start small. One sitting. Something they can finish and feel good about.

Examples:
- Write 3 captions for a recent event
- Find 2 local DJs on Instagram and message them with a script
- Attend a skate session and take 10 photos
- Add 5 merch ideas to a shared list

### Step 5: Check in

After the first task, talk to them. If it went well, give them a bigger task. If it did not, understand why and either adjust or part ways kindly.

## 8. How the owners stay above the work

Marcus and Milton should not be the people editing every video or filling every event role.

Their job is to:

- Approve the overall direction
- Hire or name Lane Leads and the Operations Coordinator
- Decide which partnerships and events to pursue
- Review money, metrics, and risks
- Remove blockers the lane leads cannot remove themselves

They are the architects. The contributors are the builders.
